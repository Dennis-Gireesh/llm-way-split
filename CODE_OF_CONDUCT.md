# Community Code of Conduct

WaySplit depends on careful technical disagreement. People should be able to
question a design, report a mistake, or ask a basic question without being
attacked or dismissed. This document is an original project policy and applies
to the repository, issue tracker, review discussions, project chat, events, and
public interactions made on the project's behalf.

## Expected conduct

Project participants are expected to:

- discuss the work without demeaning the person doing it;
- give specific, actionable feedback and state uncertainty honestly;
- make room for different backgrounds, communication styles, and levels of
  experience;
- respect privacy, consent, and boundaries, especially when examples involve
  household or financial data;
- correct misleading claims and disclose relevant conflicts or provenance;
- accept a maintainer's request to pause an escalating exchange; and
- repair harm where possible after making a mistake.

Technical decisions should be supported by evidence, reproducible behavior, or
clearly identified judgment. Disagreement with a proposal is welcome. Repeatedly
redirecting discussion toward a contributor's identity, motives, intelligence,
or worth is not.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following behavior is not accepted:

- harassment, threats, stalking, intimidation, or unwanted sexual attention;
- discriminatory or degrading language, images, jokes, or exclusion;
- personal insults, sustained hostility, deliberate humiliation, or inciting a
  group to target someone;
- publishing or threatening to publish private information without permission;
- pressuring anyone to disclose a real statement, credential, identity, or
  private security report;
- impersonation, deceptive attribution, or knowingly false provenance claims;
- retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith safety or conduct concern;
  or
- continuing contact after a clear request to stop.

Good-faith security research and firm review feedback are not harassment when
they remain scoped to the work, minimize harm, and follow the project's private
reporting process.

## Reporting a concern

Send conduct reports through a private contact method listed on a maintainer's
GitHub profile. If no private method is available, open a neutral issue asking
for a private reporting channel and include no sensitive details. Security
vulnerabilities should follow `SECURITY.md` instead.

A useful report identifies the relevant place and time, what happened, available
links or records, immediate safety concerns, and any requested boundary. Do not
collect or distribute unrelated personal information in order to make a report.

## Response and enforcement

Maintainers will review reports as privately and impartially as practical. They
may ask affected people or witnesses for additional context, but will share
details only as needed to investigate and act. A maintainer who is directly
involved in a report should not make the final enforcement decision when another
qualified maintainer is available.

Responses are chosen according to impact, pattern, intent, cooperation, and risk
of recurrence. They may include clarification, a private warning, removal of
content, limits on participation, a temporary suspension, or a permanent ban.
Urgent protective action may occur before a full review. Knowingly false or
retaliatory reports may themselves violate this policy; an unproven good-faith
report does not.

Maintainers may publish a minimized outcome when community safety requires it.
They will avoid publishing a reporter's identity or sensitive evidence without
permission unless required by law.

## Maintainer responsibility

Maintainers are responsible for applying this policy consistently within project
spaces they control. They may remove contributions or restrict participation
that undermine a safe, technically productive community, and should explain an
enforcement decision to the affected person when doing so would not create
additional risk.
